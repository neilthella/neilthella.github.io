Where's the curriculum from?

History

"the interest shouldn't be contained to this garage"

When I was younger, a few friends of friends asked for some help with LEGO Robotics, since they were interested in starting their own team and wanted a structured introduction from people who had participated. What started as a tiny network to assist friends quickly became a small, well-known neighborhood brand hosting sessions year after year to interested kids. As time went on, I began to realize that these camps were about fighting two fundamental constraints (time and resources) to create a meaningful experience with transferrable knowledge so they could be prepared for running a real team. Therefore, as workshops repeated and I developed a stronger sense for how different members of a diverse set of students approach problems and process information, I continued adjusting the curriculum until it became versatile enough that students with no interest or experience in math and students who had already done LEGO robotics could find meaning from the experience.



For many of the camps I organized, I managed the logistical details (rosters, legal, communications) and lesson plans/curriculum while my friend Rohan secured the venues and funding, claiming those under his host organization STEMPower, where you can find a more expansive image and impact gallery.
